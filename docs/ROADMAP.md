## Next
0. **The instrument can answer the campaign question now, and the answer is "not for survival".** The scripted player plays a real build order after three bot bugs were fixed (workshop asked for the shelter's own building, a strict if/else chain that skipped the towers when no building was searched yet, and an `hp > 0` filter that excluded every unconverted building), and A* stopped expanding the whole grid for unreachable destinations (400 s → 13 s per slow seed). Measured over twelve nights, seeds 1–60: **24/60 with the research tree and 25/60 without** — a dead heat on wins, and a different ending in every win, because the tree is what turns survival into a cure. The tree therefore buys the ending, not the survival; making it matter defensively is a content decision that now has an instrument to measure it. Details in [BALANCE.md](BALANCE.md) and [PERF.md](PERF.md).
1. **Balance with real play.** Follow [PLAYTEST.md](PLAYTEST.md) to collect human playtests and set a target win rate per difficulty. The scripts can already craft ammo and build an economy, and the measurements say ammo is **not** the bottleneck: difficulty has to come from the early economy, structural defence or nights 3–4, not from the last horde (see [BALANCE.md](BALANCE.md)). Any claim smaller than ten points needs 60–100 seeds per arm.
2. **Region overlays** (logistics, power).
3. **Real maps, next steps.** Done: multipolygon buildings with courtyards, street widths from OSM's own tags, and **water and railways** (a second, optional Overpass request: lakes, rivers mapped as areas with islands, rivers and canals mapped as lines, and `rail`/`light_rail`; the grid gets `CellKind.Water`, which nobody crosses or builds on, and `CellKind.Rail`, which is walkable but not buildable; a street across water is a bridge and stays a street; tunnels, culverts and trams are ignored). Open: a map view (MapLibre) to pick the area by dragging, a road graph for long trips on large areas, an offline importer for bigger regions, and the sea (`natural=coastline` is not read), bridge decks with parapets, and a crossing bar where a street meets a railway.
4. **Logistics and policies.** Done: a full store now refuses the harvest instead of losing it silently (`stats.wasted`, `alert.storageFull`, a column in the ledger), a `haul` task moves the excess of a store above `policies.haulAbove` into one with room, the policy is in the drawer, and the task queue has a panel with priority arrows. Open: "keep N food" (there is nothing yet that spends food outside meals, so the policy would either starve the settlement or do nothing), and a haul that pulls from a *production* site rather than from another store.
5. **The research tree, deepened.** Twelve nodes in four branches shipped with decision 25 — preserves, cistern, rations (survival); scaffolding, forge, salvage (construction); armory, marksmanship, floodlights (defence); serum, lab, cure (society) — so the tree itself is no longer the work. What is missing is the branch the game has no system for yet: **energy** waits on the power network (item 6), and the other four stop at four nodes each, which is two meaningful choices per branch, not a tree a player plans a run around. Deepen after the playtest says which branches are actually being taken.
6. **Energy and water networks**, vehicles.
7. **Performance: measured, and closed except for the one thing that needs hardware.** The simulation is under 1% of a frame with a horde (0.164 ms per tick with 256 infected), a 1.2 km map with 1,553 buildings builds in 845 ms and draws in 19 calls, and the two structural items — a worker thread and aggregated distant hordes — are rejected on those numbers rather than deferred. What remains is the 60 fps budget on a real GPU, which has never been measured, and which no local optimisation substitutes for. Details in [PERF.md](PERF.md).
8. **Settlements and diplomacy:** the "rebuild society" stage.

- [x] **HUD and minimap.** Inline SVG resource icons, horde entry markers on the minimap after dusk, and a rendered survivor portrait in the side panel.
- [x] **A pipeline with a contract.** `manifest.json` with size and `sha256`; `pnpm assets:check` re-derives byte budget, PNG header, alpha channel and GLB header from disk and runs in CI.
- [x] **`tools/effectcraft/bake.sh`.** One command from recipe to shipped atlas: it builds the comp, renders the PNG sequence, renames EffectCraft's literal `f_%05d_00000.png` pattern into something ffmpeg reads, keeps every Nth frame from an optional offset when a sheet ships part of the render, reports per-frame alpha coverage, packs the sheet, and prints the atlas sha256 against the one read *before* anything wrote. `pnpm assets:vfx <recipe> <atlas_name> --cell N --cols N --rows N [--src N] [--every N] [--offset N]`. Proven by rebuilding the strike and the muzzle flash from their recipes and matching the shipped bytes exactly (`961a76cd…`, `29f82024…`) with `git status` left empty, and by refusing a wrong grid before writing anything (exit 1). Its counter-example is the noise ring: its recipe renders 30 frames while the shipped sheet is 8, and no `--every 4 --offset N` reproduces its bytes — `--offset 1` is refused outright because the last sampled frame is empty — so that recipe no longer describes how that atlas was made. Coverage matches to within 0.3 points, so it is visually the same sheet; the point is that the tool says so instead of shipping a different one. Two mistakes it owns, both made by hand once: the literal filename pattern, and a `--generator` that duplicated the builder's own suffix. A third was caught in review of the tool itself: comparing the new sha with the manifest *after* the packer rewrote it always answers "reproducible".
- [x] **A flipbook per event.** Shots play a white-hot muzzle flash over a shock ring, deaths a cloud of dust and dried blood, work ticks pale dust, infected strikes four claw streaks raked over an impact flash, gunfire a ring spreading over the ground to the radius that pulls infected (45 m pistol, 70 m rifle), and a fallen wall, tower or gate a dome of rubble dust with masonry thrown up, all rendered in EffectCraft from recipes in `tools/effectcraft/`; the spark is left to hits. Clouds blend normally and replace the particles of their event, the strike replaces its particles too and mirrors every other slash, noise rings lie flat and merge when shots come from within 10 m (three alive at most), collapses are capped at four, and construction dust is capped at six alive so a busy site never takes the slots shots and deaths need (decision 21).
- [x] **`tools/make-flipbook-atlas.ts`.** Packs a raw RGBA sequence into an atlas, rejects an empty frame and records grid and per-frame coverage in the manifest. Soft effects ship at 96 px cells or every other frame when 128 px would cost more than 100 KB.
- [x] **The strike was the weakest flipbook, and now it is not.** Each of the seven effects was measured with one camera, a paused simulation and a baseline of two identical frames (0.00% of the pixels differ) against an empty effect pool, at the combat zoom (60 m) and the default one (170 m). The old three-stroke sheet owned 0.04% and 0.01% of the frame with a mean contrast of 89 and 45 — the least of the six atlas effects at both distances, on the event the player sees on every infected strike. `tools/effectcraft/melee_swipe.build.json` was rebuilt with four thick staggered claws over an impact flash and embers: peak cell coverage 9.4% → 52.7%, every frame carrying content (the old sheet started at 2.9% and ended at 2.5%, so it blinked out mid-life), 59 KB in a 5 by 2 sheet, `MELEE_SIZE` 3.4 → 4.0 m and the life 0.24 → 0.30 s to match ten frames at 30 fps. The strike now owns 0.21% and 0.03% with contrast 140 and 131: fifth and third of the seven, up from last. Capture in `docs/art/melee.png` (`MELEE_SHOT=1`). The same measurement had put the muzzle flash last and it was rebuilt next, in the bullet below; the run-to-run noise of the method is about 15% at the combat zoom, measured by leaving the spark untouched and reading it twice (0.29% and 0.25%).
- [x] **The muzzle flash follows.** It fires on every single shot and owned 0.07% of the frame at 60 m with a mean contrast of 119 — the least of the seven once the strike was fixed. `tools/effectcraft/muzzle_flash.build.json` was rebuilt from two orange stars over a small core into a white-hot core, a six-point burst, a nine-point flare and a shock ring that keeps growing as the flare collapses: peak cell coverage 30.5% → 57.2%, first frame 18.6% → 24.8% (so the flash never starts empty), six frames instead of four, 75 KB. `MUZZLE_SIZE` 2.6 → 3.0 m and the life 0.11 → 0.13 s to match six frames at 30 fps. It now owns 0.16% with contrast 136 at the combat zoom (2.3 times the area) and at the default zoom the counted area is unchanged at 0.02% while the contrast rises from 96 to 128 and the box grows from 24×17 to 26×20 px: a flash that brief is a punctuation, and being legible at 170 m is its ceiling. Capture in `docs/art/muzzle.png` (`MUZZLE_SHOT=1`). The weakest by area is now the construction dust at 0.15% / 0.02%, which is deliberately quiet and capped at six alive.
- [x] **Textured ground keeps its colour.** The ground materials kept their flat colour under the map and were darkened twice (asphalt at sRGB 10 instead of 52); each now takes a tint derived from its map's mean, pinned by a test.
- [x] **Facades, roofs and a parapet.** `tools/blender/facades.py` bakes four facade modules in Cycles from real geometry (residential brick with boarded windows, office ribbon glazing, corrugated industrial, a storefront with roller shutters for the ground floor of shops) and a tar roof with vents, each with a lit-window mask; `tools/pack-facades.ts` ships them as JPEG (110 KB for all nine). - [x] **Sidewalks, curbs and doors.** Blocks wear baked concrete slabs (tinted, cracked, mossy joints, the odd one missing) and a curb at the edge; imported maps get a sidewalk ribbon along every road. Close capture in `docs/art/city_street.png`.
- [x] **Rooftop kit.** `tools/blender/roof_props.py` makes five pieces (water tank, AC unit, stair bulkhead, antenna, mushroom vent; 48–264 triangles, 41 KB in all), and `placeRoofProps` dresses each roof by its building: bulkheads from three floors up, tanks on homes, AC units on shops and offices, vents and the odd antenna anywhere, inside the parapet, apart from each other and square with the first wall. Same instanced loader as the street kit: one draw call per piece and material.
- [x] **Units walk instead of sliding.** Both models ship six baked poses of a stride plus a standing pose (`pose0`…`pose6`) from `tools/blender/survivors.py` and `infected.py`, and the renderer picks one per unit from the distance it has travelled — a unit that has not moved gets the standing pose, because freezing it on the contact pose left the squad permanently mid-lunge with its feet 0.9 m apart (measured model depth 0.947 m walking against 0.491 m standing) — the planted foot stays with the ground, where a clock-driven phase slides whenever the simulation changes speed. No skeleton: `InstancedMesh` cannot carry one and one `SkinnedMesh` per unit would cost a draw call per survivor. Costs `poses x materials` draw calls: measured 52 standing and 75 with thirty-three infected spread over the seven poses, against 1,141 KB → 1,372 KB of art (survivor 21 → 123 KB, infected 28 → 157 KB). The first attempt drove the cycle with `sin(2πi/6)` and produced two distinct poses out of six — `sin(60°) == sin(120°)` — so the cycle is a table of six gait keys; the exporter prints each pose's foot positions and its distance from pose 0 to prove they differ (0.26 m to 0.97 m). Survivors had no model script at all before this: `walker.glb` came from a one-off experiment. Seeing them walk also exposed the unit scale: at 1.9× life size a survivor was 3.65 m tall against a 1.41 m car, so units are now drawn at 1.0 and the default camera distance moved from 170 m to 120 m to keep them readable (decision 9). `tools/blender/infected.py` builds one hunched silhouette (504 triangles, 28 KB, three materials) and the renderer instances it in place of the capsule, three draw calls for the whole horde. The kind is not modelled: `INFECTED[kind].scale` and `.color` already separate walker, runner and brute, and the skin is the only part that takes the tint, so a horde reads at distance without three meshes to keep in sync. The eyes are the only emissive surface, which is what makes them visible after dark. The capsule stays in the scene and is hidden only once the GLB lands, so a missing file costs a silhouette and nothing else. `tests/e2e/infected-shot.spec.ts` asserts the swap; `INFECTED_SHOT=1` captures `docs/art/infected.png` (decision 23).
- [x] **Frame cost probe.** `GameController.cost` and `tests/e2e/perf.spec.ts` split simulation from render; results and their limits in [PERF.md](PERF.md).

Two constraints learned the hard way still apply to every new asset:

- **No video with alpha reaches either engine.** Godot 4.7.2 has no `VideoStreamWebm`, and an OGV transcode loses alpha (`Requested planes not available`). Anything animated goes PNG sequence → atlas → flipbook, never a video file.
- **No asset enters the repository unverified.** Every asset is listed in `manifest.json` and checked by `pnpm assets:check`; a render once claimed by an agent was fabricated and left no file on disk.

## Done — characters rebuilt on a skeleton

- [x] **One continuous body, posed by a skeleton, baked to morphs.** `tools/blender/characters.py` authors the body once as lofted rings bound to eleven bones, two bones per ring across a joint, so a knee or an elbow bends as skin; the old figure was rigid segments, six sides to a limb. Legs are solved by two-bone inverse kinematics from a foot path on the ground, so a planted foot stays planted; spine, arms and head are posed from angles. Survivors wear an open jacket, a pack with a bedroll, straps, a belt and a pouch; the infected are hunched, with a torn sleeve, a bare foot, a slack jaw and dried stains. 1,194 and 988 vertices, 486 KB and 401 KB, both under the 512 KB ceiling.
- [x] **Gaits that cover their ground.** Eight walk keys and twelve run keys (eight flattened the knee, where the shin swings a right angle in an eighth of a cycle), with heel strike, toe-off, pelvis bob, sway and counter-rotation, and arms swinging against their legs. Each gait's stance sweeps exactly the distance the renderer advances per cycle (asserted in the generator), and the survivor's cycle went from 1.65 m to 2.5 m: at 3.1 m/s that is 2.5 steps a second, a jog, where it used to be 3.8, a scurry. The infected walker drags its right leg; the runner claws forward.
- [x] **Standing is not freezing.** Idle blends breathing with an occasional shift of weight and a look over the shoulder; being hit has its own pose; starting, stopping and turning ease in. Trousers and hair take a colour per person as jackets and skin already did, and the infected's kind colour is pulled most of the way to a corpse's pallor, so the horde is no longer neon. Captures in `docs/art/characters.png` and `characters_attack.png` (`CHARACTER_SHOT=1`); pose sheets from `tools/blender/characters_preview.py`.

## Budget

Every art item has to be measured against a declared ceiling, otherwise "looks better" wins every argument:

- **Initial payload ≤ 1.5 MB.** The JS bundle is 976 kB raw and 276 kB gzip (2026-11-05, up from 869/245 kB before the art pass). Art is not part of it: every map loads after the first frame and the renderer keeps its flat colours until it lands.
- **Streamed art ≤ 8 MB, 512 KB per file** (raised from 1 MB on 2026-11-06 for the city pass). `public/art` is 5546 KB after the six flipbooks, the facade, sidewalk and door bake, the rooftop kit, the two unit models with their walk poses; `pnpm assets:check` enforces both numbers. Atlases are shared and tiled, and nothing ships per object.
- **60 fps at 1080p** with about 100 buildings, forty survivors and one night horde. Shadows, bloom and particle counts are the three knobs that move this number.
- **Audio and large textures stay out of the bundle:** sound streams from `public/sfx` (372 KB), and no texture over 64 kB is inlined.
- An asset that cannot be measured against these numbers does not ship.
